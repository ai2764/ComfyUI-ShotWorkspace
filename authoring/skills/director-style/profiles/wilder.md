# Wilder-inspired wit with a human cost — v2.2.4

Mechanism: two people pursue different immediate advantages; a reply turns an earlier practical claim against its speaker, then reveals what the bargain costs emotionally.

## Dialogue and listening

With LOCKED words, find where the advantage changes. Play the opening with conversational confidence; let the answer land before the speaker revises their next tactic. Keep enough pace for a live exchange, but do not rush articulation or cover a response with the next line. Amusement can stay private; no audience-facing wink.

## Show the change

Use shared geography while both compete. Isolate a listening reaction or admission only if the audience learns something unavailable in the shared view. Return to the shared space when the bargain changes. A concrete final act should make the humane choice visible. Avoid nonstop quips, insult matches, explanatory epigrams and stock setup/punchline rhythm in every pair of cues.




## Concrete scene study: The Apartment (1960), the concluding card game
Wilder and Diamond return to a modest shared activity rather than closing on an explanatory romantic speech. A practical response carries the emotional decision. Study how an ordinary action or verbal rule changes meaning after vulnerability has been revealed. Adapt that economy to the new facts; do not copy source dialogue, force a card game, or add a tragic history. Source: Billy Wilder's explanation in Cameron Crowe's 1999 interview, Conversations with Billy, Vanity Fair.
