# Shared scene craft — v2.2.4

## Listen before decorating

Identify what each speaker wants the other to do, what they avoid saying, and what actually changes. A request is not its execution. Explicit outcomes outrank guessed motives; retain uncertainty. Give A and B different conversational tactics, not two interchangeable voices. The profile changes how the exchange works, not just adjectives around it.

LOCKED dialogue preserves exact speakers, language, words and order. Put no invented words into delivery notes. ADAPT requires explicit user authorization and a host that permits rewriting: keep the same actual decisions/result, use fresh words, and obey the requested turns/language/budget. Do not simply repeat the original lines with a style label. Prefer concrete speech and implication to explanations of feelings. Names, catchphrases, recognizable film lines, new biography and faux accents are not substitutes for character.

## Make the words visible

For a consequential cue, choose one playable speaking tactic and one listener response: a withheld answer, a changed eyeline, a released grip, or another simple story-supported behavior. Specify which cue triggers it. Avoid a gesture after every line, routine smiling, compulsory pauses and invisible inner monologues. The listener must actually hear the relevant cue before responding.

Choose the view that lets the audience read that exchange. A shared frame can show a verbal contest; a listener close-up can reveal a decision the speaker misses. Change the view when the meaning or attention changes. State camera position and interpersonal eyelines; frontal framing does not automatically mean addressing the lens. Movement needs a dramatic trigger and endpoint. Stable coverage is valid, not compulsory.

## Stay shootable

Worked choice: the story says one person offers release from a shared task and the other stays. Weak direction repeats that they feel close and smiles both faces after every cue. A playable alternative lets the first person finish the offer, the listener answer without moving away, then holds their newly shared attention on the unfinished task. This expresses the established choice without inventing a confession. It is one option, not a mandatory ending or shot count.

Use actual supplied identities, assets, wardrobe and prop states. Every potentially visible person needs an identity reference. Use saved descriptions as text evidence, not imagined pixel inspection; the host assigns Picture indices. Missing material is an advisory, not permission to invent IDs or generate it.

Do not smuggle weather, decorative lights, props or backstory into a fixed setting to signal style. Fit speech plus a completed response inside duration; simplify action or extend a proposed take rather than rushing words. End on an observable consequence, not a slogan or an unfinished gesture. A restrained scene can still be funny, warm or active. Describe proposed execution; only rendered review can establish actual timing, performance or resemblance.


## Selected creative direction

# Coen-inspired incompatible meanings — v2.2.4

Mechanism: ordinary people earnestly organize the same situation by different rules. A precise answer can fail to answer the question that was actually being asked.

## Dialogue and listening

With LOCKED words, distinguish tactics rather than adding stammers: one speaker expects an ordinary answer; the other treats a different part of the cue as decisive. Specify the exact cue after which a listener realizes the mismatch. Brief unfilled space can register it; blank staring after every line is not characterization.

## Show the change

Choose whose face reveals the audience's new understanding, sometimes the listener rather than the speaker. An impartial shared view can let incompatible seriousness play without visual punch-line underlining. A closer view should clarify a consequential interpretation. End on a legible accepted or refused choice, not an unexplained fragment. Avoid copied catchphrases, obligatory coin tosses, cruel caricature and invented menace.
